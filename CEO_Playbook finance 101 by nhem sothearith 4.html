What Is The CEO Playbook?

The CEO Playbook: Finance 101 by Nhem Sothearith is an educational website designed to introduce students and future business leaders to the fundamentals of business, finance, leadership, and decision-making.

The website explains important business concepts using simple language and practical examples. Visitors can learn how businesses manage cash, understand profit, attract investors, evaluate assets and liabilities, and lead teams.

The goal is to make business fundamentals easier to understand and encourage young people to develop their financial knowledge, critical thinking, and leadership skills.

PILLAR 01

Cash Flow & Capital

What Is Capital?

Capital is the initial bucket of money that you need to start a business. You use capital to buy your first laptops, rent an office, or hire your first team members.

Without capital, a company cannot easily be launched.

What Is Cash Flow?

Cash flow is the movement of money into and out of your business every single day.

Inflow: Money coming into your company, such as customers buying your product.

Outflow: Money leaving your company, such as paying rent or electricity bills.

The Golden CEO Rule

A CEO should monitor how much money flows in and out. If the business spends more cash than it receives for too long, it may run out of cash and struggle to pay its bills.

PILLAR 02

Profit vs Revenue

The Big Confusion

Many people think that making a lot of sales means making a lot of money. This is a huge mistake. Successful business leaders know the difference between revenue and profit.

What Is Revenue?

Revenue is the total money your company takes in from sales before subtracting costs.

Example: If your company sells 100 T-shirts for $10 each, your revenue is $1,000.

What Is Profit?

Profit is the money left after paying all your business costs.

Example: If the T-shirts, shipping, and packaging cost $600, subtract that amount from your revenue.

Revenue − Costs = Profit
$1,000 − $600 = $400
The CEO Lesson

Revenue is just a vanity number, and profit is sanity. You can make millions in revenue, but if your costs are higher than your revenue, you make no profit and your company may fail.

PILLAR 03

Equity & Investors

What Is Equity?

Equity means ownership. When you start a company by yourself, you may own 100% of it. You own all the pieces of the pie.

Why Do CEOs Sell Equity?

If you want your company to grow fast, you may need more capital to build offices or buy large amounts of inventory. If you do not have enough money, you can ask an investor for help.

How It Works

Step 1: An investor gives your company $50,000 in cash.

Step 2: In return, you give the investor 20% equity, or ownership, in your company.

Step 3: You own 80% of the company and the investor owns 20%, assuming these are the only two owners after the investment.

Founder: 80% | Investor: 20%
The Billionaire Secret

A founder's ownership share may become very valuable if the company grows significantly. However, growth is never guaranteed, and selling equity means sharing ownership of the company.

PILLAR 04

Assets vs Liabilities

Evaluating the true financial health of an enterprise requires analyzing what the organization owns versus what it owes.

What Are Assets?

Assets are valuable things a company owns or controls, such as cash, equipment, and inventory. They can help the business operate and earn money.

What Are Liabilities?

Liabilities are financial obligations or debts that a business owes, such as loans and unpaid bills.

Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities

Example: Two Students, Two Lemonade Stands

Student 1

Assets:

$100 cash in the register

$50 worth of lemons and sugar

Total Assets: $150

Liabilities:

$200 borrowed from parents to buy a fancy electronic sign

Total Liabilities: $200

$150 − $200 = −$50

Result: Student 1 has a negative net worth of $50. The stand looks cool, but its debt is greater than its assets.

Student 2

Assets:

$80 cash in the register

$20 in wooden signs they made

Total Assets: $100

Liabilities:

$0 borrowed because everything was paid for upfront

Total Liabilities: $0

$100 − $0 = $100

Result: Student 2 has a positive net worth of $100. The stand is smaller, but the student has no debt in this example.

Takeaway for Future CEOs

Don't judge a company by its fancy looks! Compare what a business owns with what it owes. Strong assets and manageable liabilities can support financial stability. Debt can be useful, but too much debt can create risk.

PILLAR 05

Leadership & Decision-Making

Leading the Business

What Do CEOs Actually Do?

Leadership and decision-making are important responsibilities of a CEO. A CEO leads the business, sets its direction, and makes important decisions. CEOs also work with experts in areas such as finance, marketing, operations, and human resources. These experts provide information and advice that helps the CEO make informed decisions for the business.

What Makes a CEO a Good Leader?

Good leadership is not about shouting orders. It is about giving the team a clear direction, trusting them to do their part, and taking responsibility when things go wrong. Great CEOs listen more than they talk, explain the "why" behind every big move, and celebrate the team's wins instead of taking all the credit.

How CEOs Lead Their Teams Toward Success

A CEO steers the company the way a captain steers a ship: by setting the direction and delegating the details to the right experts. Finance experts watch the cash, marketing experts find customers, operations experts keep things running, and human resources teams take care of employees.

When every expert reports honestly to the CEO, the CEO can make informed decisions that move the whole business forward.

The Leadership Secret

A CEO does not need to be the smartest person in every room — only the one who listens best, decides bravely, and points everyone at the same goal.

Five Habits of a Great CEO Leader

01. Listen First

Great CEOs hear their team before they speak their own mind.

02. Communicate Clearly

Everyone should know the goal in one simple sentence.

03. Decide with Courage

A wrong-but-fixed decision can be better than a decision that is never made. Learn from mistakes and correct the course when necessary.

04. Lead by Example

The team copies the habits of the boss — good ones or bad ones. Set a positive example through actions.

05. Trust the Experts

Delegating is not weakness; it is how big companies use different people's skills to get things done.
